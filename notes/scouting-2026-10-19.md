# Scouting notes, 19 October 2026

The research gathered for this project during Foundation0's
[deep-fields scouting](https://github.com/Foundation-0/f0/tree/main/deep-fields) of 18–19 October 2026. Facts in the
first section were checked against their sources on 19 Oct 2026. Facts in the second come from the scouting report
and have not been re-checked, so check one before you build on it.

**Question.** Does the record covering system with minimum modulus 42 check out, and is there one with minimum
modulus 43? This is [Erdős problem 2](https://www.erdosproblems.com/2).

**Hub.** [math0](https://github.com/Foundation-0/math0).

## Checked on 19 Oct 2026

- **The problem page.** [erdosproblems.com/2](https://www.erdosproblems.com/2): "The best known lower bound is a
  covering system with minimum modulus 42, due to Owens [Ow14]". Balister et al. [BBMST22] "improved the upper bound
  on the smallest modulus to 616000".
- **The source of 42.** Tyler Owens, "A Covering System with Minimum Modulus 42", MS thesis, Brigham Young
  University, December 2014 ([BYU ScholarsArchive](https://scholarsarchive.byu.edu/etd/4329)).

Added while setting up this repository, also checked on 19 Oct 2026:

- **Who proved the bound first.** The same problem page credits Hough [Ho15] with the first proof that the smallest
  modulus cannot be arbitrarily large, with a bound of 10<sup>16</sup>: B. Hough, "Solution of the minimum modulus
  problem for covering systems", Annals of Mathematics (2015), 361–382. It calls the proof by Balister, Bollobás,
  Morris, Sahasrabudhe and Tiba "an alternative, simpler, proof": "On the Erdős covering problem: the density of the
  uncovered set", Inventiones Mathematicae (2022), 377–414.
- **Distinct moduli.** The problem is about covering systems whose moduli are all different. Without that rule the
  question is trivial, since all the remainders on division by one large number cover every integer.
  formal-conjectures states it that way: "Erdős asked whether the smallest modulus in a distinct covering system can
  be arbitrarily large"
  ([ErdosProblems/2.lean](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/main/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/2.lean)).
- **Nielsen's paper.** Pace P. Nielsen, "A covering system whose smallest modulus is 40", Journal of Number Theory
  129 (2009), 640–666 ([doi:10.1016/j.jnt.2008.09.016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jnt.2008.09.016)). Title and
  details checked against Crossref.
- **Others on the problem.** The problem page lists one forum user as working on the problem and one as formalising
  it.

## From the scouting report, not yet re-checked

- No journal version of Owens's thesis and no independent verification of 42 were found.
- One paper marks the attribution "(???)" ([arXiv:1905.07386](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.07386)).
- A 2024 paper cites 42 ([arXiv:2407.15280](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.15280)).
- The previous record was Nielsen's 40 (2009).
- BYU's site blocked automated download of the thesis, so a person may need to download the PDF.
- **Method.** The certificate is the list of congruences. Two independent programs confirm that it covers every
  integer, for example by working modulo the lcm (the least common multiple of the moduli) through its prime
  factorisation with the Chinese remainder theorem, rather than trying every remainder. A checker verified in Lean
  is possible later.
- **Risks.** A system with minimum modulus 43 may need an lcm far beyond reach. If 42 turns out wrong, that is itself
  a result.

## First tasks

The first tasks are open as [issues](https://github.com/Foundation-0/covering-min-modulus/issues):

1. Get the thesis PDF and transcribe Owens's system into `systems/owens-42.txt`, with page references.
2. Write two independent coverage checkers that share no code, with tests on small known covering systems.
3. Verify Owens's 42 and Nielsen's 40 with both checkers.
4. Survey the methods behind 40 and 42, and what a search for 43 would need: the size of the lcm and a search
   strategy.
5. Once a person has checked the verification, report it to erdosproblems.com and propose a Ledger entry.
